## Chapter 3: The 8 Best Places

Each place has a quick info box: how to get there, how long to stay, and the cost.

### 1. Arakurayama Sengen Park (Chureito Pagoda)

The famous photo: a red five-story pagoda with Mt. Fuji behind it. In April it is framed by cherry blossoms, in November by red leaves.

- **Access:** **Shimoyoshida Station**, then about 10 minutes on foot. [CHECK: walking time] The Fuji Excursion from Shinjuku stops here, so you can come straight from Tokyo. From Kawaguchiko it is a short ride on the Fujikyu Railway.
- **Time needed:** 1–1.5 hours
- **Cost:** Free
- **Good to know:** You climb about 400 steps to the viewpoint. [CHECK] There is also a gentler slope path. Go early: by 10 a.m. the viewpoint is crowded.

### 2. Lake Kawaguchiko North Shore (Oishi Park)

The classic "Fuji across the lake" view. Flower fields in summer (lavender in late June–July) and red maples in autumn.

- **Access:** Red Line sightseeing bus, **"Kawaguchiko Natural Living Center (Oishi Park)"** stop, the last stop of the line (Fujikyu Bus official timetable)
- **Time needed:** 1 hour
- **Cost:** Free
- **Good to know:** The shop at the Natural Living Center sells blueberry soft-serve ice cream. [CHECK]

### 3. Oshino Hakkai

Eight spring-fed ponds of crystal-clear water from Mt. Fuji's snowmelt, in a small village with thatched-roof houses.

- **Access:** Local bus from Fujisan Station or Kawaguchiko Station, about 20–30 minutes [CHECK]
- **Time needed:** 1 hour
- **Cost:** Free to walk around (some private areas charge a small fee) [CHECK]
- **Good to know:** Very busy midday. Do not touch or throw coins into the ponds.

### 4. Mt. Fuji Panoramic Ropeway (Mt. Tenjo)

A 3-minute ride up from the shore of Lake Kawaguchiko. At the top: wide views of Mt. Fuji and the lake.

- **Access:** Red Line sightseeing bus, **"Yuransen / Ropeway Iriguchi"** stop, about 11 minutes from Kawaguchiko Station (Fujikyu Bus official timetable). Walking from the station: [CHECK]
- **Time needed:** 1 hour
- **Cost:** About ¥1,000 round trip [CHECK]
- **Good to know:** Try the "tanuki dango" (sweet rice dumplings) at the top. [CHECK]

### 5. Saiko Iyashi-no-Sato Nenba

A rebuilt village of about 20 thatched-roof houses with Mt. Fuji in the background. Inside are craft workshops, small museums and snack stands.

- **Access:** Green Line sightseeing bus, **"Saiko Iyashi-no-Sato Nenba"** stop, about 40 minutes from Kawaguchiko Station. Buses every 30 minutes; last bus from the station 16:40. (Fujikyu Bus official timetable)
- **Time needed:** 1–1.5 hours
- **Cost:** About ¥500 [CHECK]
- **Good to know:** Some workshops let you rent a kimono and take photos. [CHECK]

### 6. Fuji Subaru Line 5th Station

The highest point you can reach by road on the Yamanashi side, about 2,300 m above sea level. You are standing on Mt. Fuji itself.

- **Access:** Direct bus from Kawaguchiko Station, about 50 minutes [CHECK]
- **Time needed:** 1.5–2 hours
- **Cost:** Bus about ¥2,000–3,000 round trip [CHECK]
- **Good to know:** Open roughly mid-April to early December, closed in bad weather. [CHECK] It is 10°C or more colder than at the lake, so bring a jacket even in summer.

### 7. Fuji-Q Highland

An amusement park famous for record-breaking roller coasters, with Mt. Fuji views from the rides.

- **Access:** Fujikyu Railway, **Fuji-Q Highland Station** (one stop from Kawaguchiko) [CHECK]
- **Time needed:** Half a day to a full day
- **Cost:** Free entry; pay per ride or buy a day pass [CHECK]
- **Good to know:** Lines for the big coasters can be 1–2 hours on weekends.

### 8. Lake Motosu (the ¥1,000 bill view)

The view of Mt. Fuji printed on the back of the old ¥1,000 bill. The quietest and least crowded of the five lakes.

- **Access:** Blue Line sightseeing bus to **"Lake Motosu Tourist Information Center"** (about 1 hour from Kawaguchiko Station, adult fare ¥1,470), then **about 1 hour on foot** to the viewpoint near "Koansō" [CHECK: exact name and spelling of the viewpoint]. (Bus stop, fare and walking time confirmed by phone with Fujikyu Bus, November 2, 2026.) Buses run only about once an hour; last bus from Kawaguchiko Station 15:35, last bus back from the Tourist Information Center 16:45. (Fujikyu Bus official timetable)
- **Plan carefully:** with the 1-hour walk each way, you need about 3 hours here. Take a morning bus, and start walking back from the viewpoint by about 15:30 so you do not miss the last bus.
- **Time needed:** about 3 hours including the walk (plus bus waiting time)
- **Cost:** Free
- **Good to know:** In April–May, the Fuji Shibazakura Festival (pink moss phlox) is held nearby. [CHECK]

## Chapter 5: Local Food

### Hoto

Thick, flat wheat noodles simmered with pumpkin and vegetables in a miso soup, served bubbling in an iron pot. Warm and filling, perfect on a cold day.

- **Price:** About ¥1,200–1,800 [CHECK]
- **How to eat:** It arrives very hot. Use the small bowl to cool each portion.
- **Vegetarian?** The soup may contain fish stock. Ask: *"Dashi ni sakana wa haitte imasu ka?"* (Is there fish in the broth?)

### Yoshida Udon

Fujiyoshida's local udon: very thick and very firm. It is served in a soy-miso soup with cabbage and seasoned horse meat or beef.

- **Price:** About ¥500–800 [CHECK]
- **Good to know:** Many shops open only at lunchtime and close when the noodles run out.

### How to order

- Many restaurants use a **ticket machine** at the entrance. Pay first, give the ticket to the staff.
- Pointing at the menu photo is perfectly fine.
- Tipping is not expected in Japan.

## Chapter 6: Good to Know

### Avoiding crowds

- Arrive by 9–10 a.m. Tour buses arrive late morning.
- Visit Chureito Pagoda first, before 9 a.m. if possible.
- Weekdays are much quieter than weekends and Japanese holidays.

### Photo manners

The area has had serious problems with overtourism. Locals will appreciate it if you:

- Do not stand in the road or block traffic for photos.
- Do not enter private property or shops just to take pictures.
- Keep your voice down in residential areas.
- Take your trash with you. There are very few public bins in Japan.

### Money, toilets and luggage

- **Cash:** Small shops and local buses may be cash only. Bring ¥5,000–10,000 in cash. [CHECK]
- **Toilets:** Free, clean toilets at stations, parks and convenience stores.
- **Luggage:** Coin lockers at Kawaguchiko Station. [CHECK: size and price]

## Chapter 3: The 8 Best Places

Each place has a quick info box: how to get there, how long to stay, and the cost.

### 1. Arakurayama Sengen Park (Chureito Pagoda)

The famous photo: a red five-story pagoda with Mt. Fuji behind it, framed by cherry blossoms in spring. Cherry blossoms are best around **mid-April** and autumn leaves around **mid-November** (approximate; it changes every year). (Confirmed by phone with Fujiyoshida Kanko Shinko Service, November 3, 2026.)

- **Access:** **Shimoyoshida Station**, then **about 25 minutes on foot** to the pagoda (Chureito), including the stairs. (Fujiyoshida City Tourist Guide, official site, checked November 2026.) The Fuji Excursion from Shinjuku stops here, so you can come straight from Tokyo. From Kawaguchiko it is a short ride on the Fujikyu Railway.
- **Time needed:** 1–1.5 hours
- **Cost:** **Free** to enter. Only parking costs money: the nearest car park (P1) is ¥1,500 for up to 6 hours. (Confirmed by phone with Fujiyoshida Kanko Shinko Service, November 3, 2026.)
- **Open:** 24 hours. Night illumination: Jan–Feb 16:00–21:50, Mar–Apr 17:00–21:50, May 18:00–21:50, Jun–Sep 19:00–21:50, Oct–Nov 17:00–21:50, Dec 16:00–21:50 (times may change).
- **Good to know:** The viewing deck is at the top of **398 steps**. (Fujiyoshida City Tourist Guide, official site, checked November 2026.)
- **Toilets:** available at the park. The park is now very busy with visitors from overseas, and cars are asked not to drive up to the pagoda square, so walk up from Shimoyoshida Station. (Fujiyoshida City Tourist Guide, official site)

### 2. Lake Kawaguchiko North Shore (Oishi Park)

The classic "Fuji across the lake" view, with flowers from spring to autumn. It is famous for lavender.

| Flower | Usually at its best |
|---|---|
| Nemophila (blue) | mid-April – late May |
| Roses | late May – mid-June |
| **Lavender** | **late June – mid-July** |
| Casablanca lilies | early – late July |
| Kochia (green) | early August – mid-October |
| **Kochia (red)** | **early – late October** |

Dates change every year; the official site posts the current flower status. (Fujikawaguchiko Furusato Shinko Foundation official site, checked November 2026.)

- **Access:** Red Line sightseeing bus, **"Kawaguchiko Natural Living Center (Oishi Park)"** stop, the last stop of the line (Fujikyu Bus official timetable)
- **Time needed:** 1 hour
- **Cost:** the Kawaguchiko Natural Living Center is free to enter, and parking is free. (Foundation official site) The park itself is **free and open at all times**, with no closing days. (Fujikawaguchiko Town Tourism Federation official site, checked November 2026.)
- **Facilities:** the Kawaguchiko Natural Living Center, the "Ooishiya" farm produce stand, a gazebo, a playground and a large parking lot. **Toilets (wheelchair accessible) are inside the Natural Living Center.** (Fujikawaguchiko Town official site, checked November 2026.)
- **Please note:** there are **no trash bins** in the park, so take your trash with you. Dogs must be on a leash. (Fujikawaguchiko Town official site)
- **Snacks:** soft-serve ice cream at "Oishi Park Cafe" (12 flavors, all ¥550 as of November 2026; **blueberry and lavender** are the regular flavors, others change with the season). Local Oishi blueberry juice ¥800 at the "Mt. Fuji View Cafe". Upstairs, "Kitchen Fujiyama View" has a big window facing Mt. Fuji and sells "Cremia" premium soft-serve, Mt. Fuji–shaped ice candy and roasted sweet potatoes (¥500). Prices and menus change. (Foundation official site, checked November 2026.)
- **Souvenirs:** Mt. Fuji goods, Yamanashi crafts and handmade blueberry jam.
- **Natural Living Center hours:** open every day. November–February: shop 9:00–17:15, cafes and soft-serve 9:15–16:15, Kitchen Fujiyama View 9:00–16:30. Other months the cafes stay open until 16:45 and the Kitchen until 17:00. Phone 0555-76-8230. (Foundation official site)

### 3. Oshino Hakkai

Eight spring-fed ponds of crystal-clear water. Snow that falls on Mt. Fuji soaks into the ground and is filtered through layers of lava for **decades** before it comes up here. The ponds were once sacred: pilgrims purified themselves here before climbing Mt. Fuji. Oshino Hakkai is a **Natural Monument of Japan** (1934), one of Japan's **100 best waters** (1985), and part of the **Mt. Fuji World Cultural Heritage** site (2013). (Oshino Village Tourism Association official site, checked November 2026.)

- **Access:**
  - From **Mt. Fuji Station** (Fujikyu Railway): Fujikyu route bus, about 15–25 minutes. (Oshino Village Tourism Association official access page, checked November 2026; the page gives both times.) The "Fujikko-go" bus, covered by the Mt. FUJI Passport, also runs to Oshino Hakkai.
  - Direct from **Shinjuku**: the Chuo Highway Bus to the Fuji Five Lakes continues past Fuji-Q Highland to Oshino Hakkai, about 2 hours 20 minutes in total. (Official access page)
  - From **Kawaguchiko Station** (buses start here; get off at **"Oshino Hakkai"**):
    - **Route bus:** lines **A1 / A2** (for Gotemba) or **F1 / F2** (for Yamanakako Hirano), 1–2 buses an hour. First bus from Kawaguchiko Station around **6:50**.
    - **Express bus:** about 10 minutes faster and about ¥200–300 more than the route bus. Leaves Kawaguchiko Station every hour from 9:40 to 16:40, **except 12:40**.
    - (Confirmed by phone with the Oshino Village Tourist Information Center, November 3, 2026.) Route bus fare: [CHECK]
- **Time needed:** 1 hour
- **Open:** there is no gate and no opening hours, so you can visit any time. But there are few street lights, so walking around **after dark is not recommended**. (Oshino Village Tourism Association official Q&A, checked November 2026.)
- **Cost:** free to walk around. One of the eight ponds, **Sokonuke-ike**, is inside the **Hannoki-bayashi Museum** and can only be seen when the museum is open (phone 0555-84-2587). The museum is set in the grounds of an old local family's house, with **thatched-roof farmhouses** thought to date from the 18th century, among the oldest in the Mt. Fuji area. It also has a restaurant serving hoto noodles and soba. Museum hours 9:00–17:00; restaurant 10:30–17:00 (closes early when food runs out). Closed on irregular days (bad weather, family events). Museum admission (tax included): adults (junior high and up) ¥300, children (elementary school) ¥150, small children (age 1 to kindergarten) ¥100; groups of 15 or more get ¥50 off each. Inside you can see the Watanabe family's late-18th-century farmhouse, Edo-period samurai armor and swords, old farm tools and a 1582 document from the Hojo clan. Local legend says Sokonuke-ike ("bottomless pond") is connected underground to Okama-ike: things dropped here were said to float up there. (Hannoki-bayashi official site, checked November 2026.)
- **Wheelchairs:** free wheelchair loan at the tourist information center (only 3, so book ahead: 0555-84-4221). Most ponds can be reached by wheelchair, but **Okama-ike** (down stairs) and **Shobu-ike** (rough path) cannot, and Deguchi-ike is far from the others. (Official Q&A)
- **Pets:** allowed on a leash; carry them or use a cage inside shops. (Official Q&A)
- **Winter:** snow usually falls from late November to March, and shady spots can be icy even without snow. (Official Q&A)
- **Tourist information center:** 0555-84-4222 (weekdays 9:00–17:00, weekends and holidays 9:00–16:00). (Oshino Village Tourism Association official site)
- **Rules:** you may touch the water, but **throwing coins into the ponds is prohibited**.
- **Crowds:** busiest from about **10:30 to 14:30**; quieter before and after. (Both confirmed by phone with the Oshino Village Tourist Information Center, November 3, 2026.)

### 4. Mt. Fuji Panoramic Ropeway (Mt. Tenjo)

A ropeway up from the shore of Lake Kawaguchiko. At the top: wide views of Mt. Fuji and the lake. [CHECK: ride time]

- **Access:** Red Line sightseeing bus, **"Yuransen / Ropeway Iriguchi"** stop, about 11 minutes from Kawaguchiko Station (Fujikyu Bus official timetable). Or about **15 minutes on foot** from Kawaguchiko Station.
- **Time needed:** 1 hour
- **Cost:** round trip adults ¥1,200, children ¥600; one way adults ¥800, children ¥400.
- **Hours:** change with the season; usually **8:30–17:30**. Hours are shorter on inspection days.
- (Fares, hours and walking time confirmed by phone with Mt. Fuji Panoramic Ropeway, November 3, 2026.)

### 5. Saiko Iyashi-no-Sato Nenba

A rebuilt village of thatched-roof houses with Mt. Fuji in the background. [CHECK: number of houses; what is inside]

- **Access:** Green Line sightseeing bus, **"Saiko Iyashi-no-Sato Nenba"** stop, about 40 minutes from Kawaguchiko Station. Buses every 30 minutes; last bus from the station 16:40. (Fujikyu Bus official timetable)
- **Time needed:** 1–1.5 hours
- **Cost:** high school students and adults ¥500, elementary and junior high students ¥250, preschool children free.
- **Hours:** through November 9:00–17:00 (last entry 16:30); December–February 9:30–16:30 (last entry 16:00).
- (Admission and hours confirmed by phone with Saiko Iyashi-no-Sato Nenba, November 3, 2026.)

### 6. Fuji Subaru Line 5th Station

The highest point you can reach by road on the Yamanashi side, about 2,300 m above sea level. You are standing on Mt. Fuji itself.

- **Access:** Direct Fujikyu bus from Kawaguchiko Station, about **1 hour** one way. The bus runs whenever the road is open; when Yamanashi Prefecture closes the Fuji Subaru Line (for example in bad weather or snow), the bus does not run. (Confirmed by phone with Fujikyu Bus, November 3, 2026.)
- **Time needed:** 1.5–2 hours
- **Cost:** bus **¥2,000 one way** for adults. (Confirmed by phone with Fujikyu Bus, November 3, 2026.)
- **Good to know:** check that the road is open before you go. [CHECK: road season; temperature]

### 7. Fuji-Q Highland

An amusement park famous for record-breaking roller coasters, with Mt. Fuji views from the rides.

- **Access:** Fujikyu Railway, **Fuji-Q Highland Station**, the stop just before Kawaguchiko; the Fuji Excursion stops here too. The highway bus from Busta Shinjuku also stops at Fuji-Q Highland. (Fujikyu Railway and Highway Bus.com official sites, checked November 2026.)
- **Time needed:** Half a day to a full day
- **Cost:** **entry is free**; you pay for rides (single ride tickets from machines inside the park, or a ride pass). Pass prices change by date:

| Pass | Adults (18–64) | Junior high & high school | Elementary school | Age 1+ / 65+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One-Day Pass (unlimited rides) | ¥6,000–7,900 | ¥5,500–7,400 | ¥4,400–5,100 | ¥2,100–2,600 |
| Afternoon Pass (from 13:00) | ¥4,100–6,000 | ¥3,800–5,700 | ¥3,500–4,200 | ¥1,700–2,100 |

  Buy online or at the park. No refunds or changes after purchase, and some rides are not covered by the passes. Check the monthly price calendar on the official site before you go. (Fuji-Q Highland official site, checked November 2026.)
- **Opening hours:** change every day, and **the park is closed on some days**. For example, in January 2027 it opens 9:00–19:00 on New Year holidays but mostly 10:00–17:00, and is closed on several Wednesdays and Thursdays (January 13, 14, 20, 21, 27 and 28). Some rides also close for inspection on certain days. **Always check the monthly calendar on the official site before you go.** (Fuji-Q Highland official site, January 2027 calendar, checked November 2026.)
- **Closed for construction:** the Ferris wheel (until January 31, 2027), "Tentekomai" (until late July 2027, planned) and "Red Tower" (until further notice). (Fuji-Q Highland official site)

### 8. Lake Motosu (the ¥1,000 bill view)

The view of Mt. Fuji printed on the back of the old ¥1,000 bill. The quietest and least crowded of the five lakes.

- **Access:** Blue Line sightseeing bus to **"Lake Motosu Tourist Information Center"** (about 1 hour from Kawaguchiko Station, adult fare ¥1,470), then **about 1 hour on foot** to the viewpoint near **Koansō (浩庵荘)**. Show the Japanese name to locals if you need directions. (Bus stop, fare and walking time confirmed by phone with Fujikyu Bus, November 2, 2026.) Buses run only about once an hour; last bus from Kawaguchiko Station 15:35, last bus back from the Tourist Information Center 16:45. (Fujikyu Bus official timetable)
- **Plan carefully:** with the 1-hour walk each way, you need about 3 hours here. Take a morning bus, and start walking back from the viewpoint by about 15:30 so you do not miss the last bus.
- **Time needed:** about 3 hours including the walk (plus bus waiting time)
- **Cost:** Free
- **Good to know:** every spring the **Fuji Shibazakura Festival** is held at **Fuji Motosuko Resort** near the lake: about 500,000 pink moss phlox plants in front of Mt. Fuji. In 2026 it ran from April 11 to May 24 (dates depend on the flowers and change every year). Check the official Fuji Motosuko Resort site for the dates and admission. (Fuji Motosuko Resort official site, checked November 2026.)

## Chapter 5: Local Food

### Hoto

Thick, flat wheat noodles simmered with pumpkin and vegetables in a miso soup, served bubbling in an iron pot. Warm and filling, perfect on a cold day.

- **Price:** depends on the restaurant, and prices go up from time to time. Example: at Hōtō Kura Fujinari (Kawaguchiko), the signature "Golden Hōtō" with pork or chicken is ¥1,815 (restaurant's own menu on Google, November 2026). Check the menu at the shop. Hōtō Kura Fujinari: 6931 Funatsu, Fujikawaguchiko.
- **How to eat:** It arrives very hot. Use the small bowl to cool each portion.
- **Vegetarian?** The soup may contain fish stock. Ask: *"Dashi ni sakana wa haitte imasu ka?"* (Is there fish in the broth?)

### Yoshida Udon

Fujiyoshida's local udon: very thick and very firm. It is served in a soy-miso soup with cabbage and seasoned horse meat or beef.

- **Price:** cheap. Example: at **Miura Udon** (near Shimoyoshida Station, handy after Chureito Pagoda), most bowls are up to about ¥900, and the most popular "niku udon" (horse meat and cabbage) is about ¥700. Extra noodles about ¥300. (Menu listed on Google, November 2026; prices may change.)
- **Good to know:** Many shops open only at lunchtime and close when the noodles run out.

### How to order

- Many restaurants use a **ticket machine** at the entrance. Pay first, give the ticket to the staff.
- Pointing at the menu photo is perfectly fine.
- Tipping is not expected in Japan.

## Chapter 6: Good to Know

### Avoiding crowds

- Arrive by 9–10 a.m. Tour buses arrive late morning.
- Visit Chureito Pagoda first, before 9 a.m. if possible.
- Weekdays are much quieter than weekends and Japanese holidays.

### Photo manners

The area has had serious problems with overtourism. Locals will appreciate it if you:

- Do not stand in the road or block traffic for photos.
- Do not enter private property or shops just to take pictures.
- Keep your voice down in residential areas.
- Take your trash with you. There are very few public bins in Japan.

### Money, toilets and luggage

- **Cash:** Small shops and local buses may be cash only. Bring ¥5,000–10,000 in cash. [CHECK]
- **Toilets:** Free, clean toilets at stations, parks and convenience stores.
- **Luggage:** Coin lockers at Kawaguchiko Station. [CHECK: size and price]

## Chapter 1: Before You Go

### How to use this guide

This guide is built for one thing: helping you see Mt. Fuji on a day trip from Tokyo, without wasting time on confusing transport or bad timing.

- **Chapter 2** shows you exactly how to get there and back.
- **Chapter 3** covers the 8 places worth your time.
- **Chapter 4** gives you ready-made itineraries you can follow as-is.
- **Chapters 5–6** cover food, manners and the small things that make the day go smoothly.

Every place in this guide is in the Fuji Five Lakes area (Yamanashi Prefecture), the easiest Fuji area to reach from Tokyo.

### When can you actually see Mt. Fuji?

Mt. Fuji hides behind clouds more often than most visitors expect. Timing matters more than anything else.

| Season | Chance of a clear view | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Winter (Dec–Feb) | Best | Cold, dry air. Snow-capped peak. |
| Spring (Mar–May) | Good | Cherry blossoms in April. Haze increases in May. |
| Summer (Jun–Aug) | Lowest | Rainy season and humidity. Peak often hidden. |
| Autumn (Sep–Nov) | Good | Red leaves in November. Typhoons possible in September. |

**Time of day:** Mornings are clearest. Clouds usually build up from late morning. Aim to arrive by 9–10 a.m.

## Chapter 2: Getting There from Tokyo

### Option A: Train

The **Fuji Excursion** limited express runs directly from **Shinjuku Station** to **Kawaguchiko Station**.

- Time: about 1 hour 56–58 minutes, no transfers
- Fare: ¥4,200 one way for adults (ticket + reserved-seat express fee; JR East Eki-net reference price, checked November 2026)
- Morning departures from Shinjuku include 8:30 and 9:30 (example timetable, November 2026). Check the latest timetable when you book.
- Book in advance on JR East "Eki-net". Morning trains can be almost full even on weekdays, and weekends and cherry blossom / autumn leaf season sell out fast.
- **Important:** The Fuji Excursion is only 3 cars. Between Shinjuku and Otsuki it is coupled to another express train ("Azusa" or "Kaiji"). **Make sure you board Cars 1–3**, or you will end up going somewhere else.
- JR Pass: covers only part of the route (the Fujikyu Railway section is extra). [CHECK]

**Backup option:** If the Fuji Excursion is full, take the "Kaiji" limited express from Shinjuku to **Otsuki**, then change to the Fujikyu Railway (for example the "Fujisan View Express") to Kawaguchiko. It takes about 20 minutes longer, and the Fujikyu section cannot be booked on Eki-net.

**Tip:** Sit on the right side of the train heading to Kawaguchiko for the best Fuji views near the end of the ride. [CHECK]

### Option B: Highway bus

Buses leave from **Shinjuku Expressway Bus Terminal ("Busta Shinjuku")** and arrive at **Kawaguchiko Station**.

- Time: about 1 hour 35–45 minutes without traffic
- Fare: ¥2,200 one way for adults, or ¥2,000 if you book and pay online in advance (children half price) [CHECK: confirm on official site]
- Frequency: every 10 minutes to 1 hour
- All seats reserved. Book online from one month before, up to 10 minutes before departure ("Highway Bus.com" or "Hassha Orai Net"). Weekend buses sell out.
- Traffic can add an hour on Sunday evenings and holidays.

### Train or bus? Quick comparison

| | Train (Fuji Excursion) | Highway bus |
|---|---|---|
| Time | About 2 hours, reliable | About 2 hours, traffic-dependent |
| Price | Higher | Lower |
| Comfort | More legroom, can walk around | Seat belt required, toilet on board [CHECK] |
| Best for | Tight schedules, return trip on busy days | Budget travelers, weekday trips |

**Our recommendation:** Bus there in the morning (cheaper, little traffic), train back in the evening (avoids Sunday traffic jams).

### Getting around: the sightseeing buses

From Kawaguchiko Station, retro-style sightseeing buses loop around the lakes.

| Line | Main stops | Useful for |
|---|---|---|
| Red Line (Kawaguchiko area) | Lake Kawaguchiko north shore, Oishi Park, ropeway | Chapter 3: places 2 and 4 |
| Green Line (Saiko area) | Saiko Iyashi-no-Sato Nenba | Chapter 3: place 5 |
| Blue Line (Narusawa / Aokigahara area) | Caves, Lake Motosu direction | Chapter 3: place 8 |

- Pass: a 2-day pass covering all lines costs about ¥1,500–2,000. [CHECK]
- Pay with cash or IC card (Suica / PASMO). [CHECK]
- Buses run about every 15–30 minutes. Last buses are early, often before 6 p.m. [CHECK]

**Tip:** Chureito Pagoda (place 1) and Oshino Hakkai (place 3) are **not** on these loop lines. See Chapter 3 for how to reach them.
